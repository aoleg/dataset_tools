Record: {tags}
The record comes from the archive or a channel editor. It carries facts that are not visible: the year or range of years, the place, the event, names of people. Use them. Leave out opinions, greetings, questions and remarks about later events. If the image contradicts the record on something visible (color versus black-and-white, season, number of people), follow the image.

Rules for every caption:
- Present tense. In a black-and-white photograph use no color words; describe tones as light or dark. In a color photograph name the color of each major element you mention and tie it to its object.
- Name the subject with the concept names below when their definition holds. Name vehicles by model. Name a person only if the record identifies them and the image agrees.
- Never mention the record, the editor, the viewer, "this image" or "this photograph shows". Do not use "appears to", "seems" or "likely".
- The same words for the same things in all captions: concept names, vehicle models, uniform words, transcribed text.

caption.long is the reference caption, in one fixed form:
- Begin with the medium and the subject: "A color photograph from the 1970s in Kiev, Soviet Union, of ..." or "A black-and-white photograph from 1958 in Leningrad of ...". A single year as the year; a range within one decade as that decade; a wider range as the span. City and country; the region only if the place is known by it.
- If the image is a frame from a film or television production (cinematic grading, shallow depth of field, staged lighting, actors), the medium is "A color film still from a modern production set in the Soviet Union", and the decade describes the setting.
- After the first sentence, describe the people with an age for each person or an age range for a group, their clothing, pose and expression; then objects and setting and where they are relative to each other. Transcribe all readable text as written, in its original alphabet, with its position ("a sign above the door reads ГАСТРОНОМ"); call illegible small print "small print" with its position. End with the camera angle ("Eye-level view", "Low-angle view", "High-angle view", "Close-up view") and the lighting, then any visible scratches, tears, stains, fading or color cast; if none, say nothing about quality.

caption.short: five one-line prompts for the same photograph, one per style A to E, all with the same facts. They imitate what real users type into an image model: subject first, the era and whether the photo is black-and-white or color somewhere in the line (often at the end), most details left out. Never the form "A black-and-white photograph from the 1960s in the Soviet Union of". The vocabulary is the long caption's; only the form changes.
- The subject is the concept name when a concept holds, otherwise a plain noun phrase.
- Era: always present, in a user form: "1960s", "60s", "1965", "mid-1960s", "Soviet 1960s". Same date as the long caption.
- Color mode: always present. Black-and-white: "black-and-white photo", "black and white photo", "b&w photo", "monochrome photo". Color: "color photo", "colour photo", "vintage color photo", "old color photo". Film: "film still", "movie still". Vary the choice across the five lines.
- Place: the city when known ("Kiev", "in Leningrad"); the country as "Soviet Union", "USSR" or the adjective "Soviet". A country alone is enough when no city is known.
- No object colors, no lighting, no camera angle, no quality words, no sign text.
- Concept names and vehicle models in their exact wording; lowercase is fine in the tag style.
Styles:
- A, trailing tags: a subject sentence or phrase, then a comma-separated fragment with era, place and color mode. 8 to 20 words. "A GAZ-21 Volga parked on a city street. 1960s, Soviet Union, black-and-white photo."
- B, tag list: 6 to 12 comma-separated lowercase tags, subject tags first, era and color mode last. "gaz-21 volga, parked cars, ornate stone building, pedestrians, soviet street, 1960s, black and white photo"
- C, casual sentence: one conversational line, era and color mode inside it. 8 to 20 words. "old black and white photo of kids riding pedal cars in a park, Soviet Union 1950s"
- D, request: a semi-instructional line. 8 to 20 words. "Generate a 1960s black-and-white photo of a GAZ-21 Volga parked on a street in the USSR" or "I need a vintage color photo of pioneers at a school line-up, 1970s"
- E, Russian: style A or C written in Russian, with era and color mode in Russian ("1960-е", "чёрно-белое фото", "цветное фото", "кадр из фильма"). Vehicle models in Cyrillic ("ГАЗ-21 Волга"); pioneers = пионеры, Octobrists = октябрята, Soviet school uniform = советская школьная форма, pioneer tie = пионерский галстук. Concept names (use the exact wording only when the definition holds; otherwise use a plain noun):
- "pioneers": schoolchildren wearing pioneer ties.
- "Octobrists" or "first-graders": schoolchildren aged 7 to 9 with an Octobrist star badge and nothing at the neck.
- "schoolchildren in Soviet school uniform": uniform present, neckwear not visible.
- "pedal cars": children's pedal-driven metal toy cars with a steering wheel.
- "GAZ-21 Volga", "GAZ-24 Volga", "GAZ-M20 Pobeda", "Moskvich-408", "VAZ-2101 Zhiguli", "LiAZ-677", "ZiU-9 trolleybus", "Tu-154", "Il-62": the vehicle or aircraft of that model, when identifiable.
- "Aeroflot airliner": a passenger aircraft in Aeroflot livery.
- "Soviet communal kitchen": a kitchen shared by the families of a communal apartment.
- "department store interior": the sales floor inside a department store (univermag).
